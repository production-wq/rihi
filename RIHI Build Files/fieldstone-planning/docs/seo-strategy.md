All competitor metrics and keyword figures in this document were pulled from the Ahrefs API in August 2026, United States index. Where a number is an estimate rather than a measurement, it says so.

## 1. THE FINDING THAT SHOULD SHAPE EVERY DECISION

Before the competitive analysis, the keyword data, or the phasing, one thing has to be understood, because it inverts the normal intuition about how a build like this works.

**No single keyword in this market justifies this project.**

Here is what the head terms actually look like:

| roof replacement cost rhode island | 0 | n/a | n/a |

If you built a site targeting only these seventeen terms and ranked first for every single one, you would capture roughly 1,800 monthly searches. That is not a business.

**Now look at the other half of the data.**

| Keyword | Volume/mo | Difficulty | CPC |
|---|---|---|---|
| gutter installation near me | 29,000 | 1 | $11.00 |
| tub to shower conversion | 6,400 | 3 | $7.00 |
| ice dam removal | 1,700 | 6 | $1.00 |

Three observations follow, and the whole strategy hangs off them.

**One. The volume is in generic service demand and "near me" queries, not in geo-modified head terms.** "Gutter installation near me" is 29,000 a month at difficulty 1. Google resolves those queries by user location, which means the way to win them is to have a genuinely relevant, genuinely local page for wherever the searcher happens to be standing. That is exactly what 559 city hubs and 3,913 service-by-city pages produce. The programmatic build is not a way to chase 559 small keywords. It is the mechanism for competing on the large ones.

**Two. Difficulty is effectively zero across the board.** Difficulty 0, 1, 3, 6, 0, 0, 0. The two exceptions, "roofing contractors rhode island" at 52 and "kitchen remodeling providence ri" at 53, are both distorted by a national brand in the results rather than by a strong local competitor. There is nothing to outrank here. There is only an empty index to fill.

**Three. Commercial value is extreme relative to volume.** "Roofers Worcester MA" is 20 searches a month at a $35.00 cost per click. "Roofers Hartford CT" is 40 at $25.00. "Replacement windows Rhode Island" is 200 at $25.00. A page that captures 20 clicks a month at a $35 CPC equivalent is producing roughly $700 a month in avoided paid spend, from one page, in one town, for one service. Multiply that thinly across thousands of pages and the aggregate is the entire thesis.

**The strategic conclusion.** Breadth is the product. A single beautifully optimized page cannot win this market because no single query is large enough. Thousands of genuinely local pages can, because the aggregate long tail is large, the difficulty is nil, and the per-click value is high. That is the case for building this. It is also precisely why quality control at scale is existential rather than nice to have: the same breadth that creates the opportunity is what trips scaled-content classifiers when the pages are thin.

### 2.2 The three biggest threats, and what each one teaches

**Couto Construction. DR 14, 3,027 visits, $15,294 traffic value.** The only competitor whose numbers look like deliberate investment rather than accident. 465 keywords and a high traffic value ratio means the traffic is commercial, not informational. Their weakness is purely geographic: they are a Fall River, New Bedford, East Bay corridor business and the site reflects that. They have proven content works in this market and then declined to scale it past a corridor.

**Berkeley Exteriors. DR 6, 2,237 visits, 617 keywords.** The most important data point in this entire document.

617 ranking keywords is the highest count in the market. It comes from a Domain Rating of 6, which is close to nothing. There is no link authority explaining this result. What produces it is a single `/service-areas` page containing one short paragraph about each of dozens of Connecticut towns. Naugatuck gets a sentence about its historic district. New Canaan gets a Frank Lloyd Wright reference. New Fairfield gets two named historic houses. Every paragraph closes with the same phrase about top-quality exterior building products.

There is no `/service-areas/naugatuck/` URL. There is no separate roofing page for Naugatuck. Every town shares one page, so every town competes with every other town for one ranking slot.

**This is the proof of concept, run accidentally, by a competitor, with the weakest possible execution.** Mere mentions of Connecticut town names on one shared page generate 617 keywords and 2,237 visits at DR 6. Dedicated town pages, each with seven dedicated service pages beneath them, each with real housing stock detail and a real Local Ledger, should substantially exceed that. The upside is not speculative. Somebody already demonstrated the floor.

**Capital Construction Contracting. DR 16, 896 visits, 165 keywords.** The most sophisticated site in the market and the only competitor writing genuinely local city copy. Their `/service-areas/[city]` pages reference the Battle Green in Lexington, Jason Heights in Arlington, and triple-decker window work in Somerville. That is real, and it is what this project has to beat rather than merely out-scale.

They are beatable on three axes at once. Exteriors only, so no bathrooms and no kitchens. Greater Boston only, so a few dozen towns out of 351. One page per city rather than one page per service per city, so each page competes for roofing, siding, windows, decking, and masonry simultaneously.

### 2.3 The lesson from the two high-DR failures

General Construction RI has a Domain Rating of 22 and gets **one** organic visit a month. Adam Quenneville has a DR of 30 and gets 153.

Authority without indexed relevant pages produces nothing. This should directly govern resource allocation: **do not prioritize link building in the first six months.** The bottleneck in this market is not authority, it is indexed pages that answer local commercial queries. Nobody is competing for those. Links matter later, at the point where two sites are contesting the same city-service term. Right now no one is contesting anything.

- Adam Quenneville's ice dam and roof repair content. Real topical authority in the Pioneer Valley. Lead ice dam content in Worcester County and the North Shore instead.

## 3. KEYWORD STRATEGY

### 3.1 Intent split, which determines page type

Two distinct query classes, and confusing them is the most common way programmatic builds waste pages.

**Transactional. Route to city-by-service pages.** Pattern: `[service] [city] [state]`, `[service] contractors near me`, `[service] company [city]`. The searcher wants a person to come to their house. Verified difficulty on these runs 0 to 6. They convert. They are worth $3 to $35 a click. They belong on `/locations/[state]/[city]/[service]/` and nowhere else.

**Informational. Route to blog posts and tools.** Pattern: `how much does [service] cost in [state]`, `[material] vs [material]`, `how to [task]`, `best time to [task]`. The searcher is six weeks to eighteen months from buying. Higher aggregate volume, much lower conversion, high assisted-conversion value. They belong on `/blog/[slug]/` and `/tools/[tool]/`.

**The bridge is the calculator.** A cost-guide post captures informational intent, the embedded calculator converts it to a specific number for their house, and the calculator output routes to the lead form. That path turns research traffic into leads without a hard sell, and it is why `docs/tools-spec.md` exists.

### 3.3 Secondary long tail, where the compounding actually happens

These patterns are individually tiny and collectively the largest source of traffic in the model:

- `[service] cost [city] [state]` across 559 cities and 7 services
- `[housing type] [service]`, for example "triple decker window replacement", "cape cod roof replacement", "historic district siding". Almost no competition and very high intent.
- `[problem] [city]`, for example "ice dam removal Worcester", "roof leak Providence"
- `[material] vs [material] [region]`
- `best [service] [city]`, the listicle format, which ranks fastest and gets cited by AI answer engines
- `[state program] [service]`, for example "Mass Save window rebate", "Connecticut Green Bank siding"

**Why the housing-stock patterns matter disproportionately.** "Triple decker window replacement Worcester" has almost no measurable volume in any keyword tool, and it is exactly the query a Worcester three-decker owner types. Nobody targets it because it does not show up in a volume report. This is the category of query that 559 genuinely local pages captures and no competitor does.

## 4. WHY RHODE ISLAND FIRST

The rollout does not start with the biggest market. It starts with the smallest complete one, for five reasons.

**One. It is the only state that can be finished.** Rhode Island is 39 municipalities. That is 39 city hubs plus 273 service-by-city pages, roughly 312 pages. Massachusetts alone is 351 municipalities and nearly 2,800 pages. Complete coverage of an entire state is a materially different signal to Google than partial coverage of a large one, and Rhode Island is the only state here where complete is achievable in one phase.

**Two. It indexes fast enough to be a real test.** A new domain will get roughly 312 pages crawled and indexed in three to four weeks. The same test on Massachusetts takes months, and by the time you learn the template is wrong you have 2,800 bad pages instead of 312.

**Three. The failure is survivable.** If city-by-service pages do not rank, the honest outcomes are: the template is too thin, the internal linking is wrong, or the market does not support the model. Learning any of those at 312 pages costs three weeks and a rewrite. Learning it at 3,913 pages risks the domain, because a site that trips a scaled-content classifier does not recover by trimming.

**Four. Rhode Island is the least defended market in the region.** RIHI has 67 keywords. Rhode Island Remodeling has 19. Royal Exteriors has 12. Ocean State has 3. Couto is strong but is concentrated in the East Bay and southeastern Massachusetts, which is a minority of the state's municipalities. Providence County and Kent County, which hold most of the population, are effectively open.

**Five. It builds authority for the harder phases.** By the time Massachusetts phase 4 arrives, the domain has 39 Rhode Island towns, 50 Massachusetts cities, and all 169 Connecticut towns indexed and earning links. Entering Lexington and Newton against Capital Construction from that position is a completely different proposition than entering on day one.

**One sequencing note within Phase 1.** Do not lead with the East Bay. Bristol, Warren, Barrington, Tiverton, and Little Compton sit in Couto's actual strength. Lead with Providence, Cranston, Warwick, Pawtucket, Woonsocket, East Providence, and the Kent County towns.

## 5. PHASE GATE CRITERIA

Hard gates. Advancing early is the most common way this category of project fails, because flooding a low-authority domain with pages starves crawl budget and delays the pages that would have ranked.

### Advance only when ALL of these are true

| # | Criterion | Threshold | Where to check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Time elapsed | 3 to 4 full weeks minimum since deploy | Deploy log |
| 2 | Indexation | At least 80% of the phase's city hubs indexed | GSC Pages report, not `site:` |
| 3 | Deep tier indexation | At least 60% of service-by-city pages indexed | GSC Pages report |
| 4 | Impression trend | Rising week over week, 3 consecutive weeks | GSC Performance, filtered to `/locations/` |
| 5 | Click activity | Non-zero clicks on city pages, not only service hubs | GSC Performance |
| 6 | Average position | Trending toward 20 or better on city terms | GSC Performance |
| 7 | Crawl health | No spike in "Discovered, currently not indexed" | GSC Pages, excluded reasons |
| 8 | Core Web Vitals | Still passing on mobile | GSC Core Web Vitals |
| 9 | Manual quality check | 10 random service-by-city pages pass the 60% uniqueness test | Manual review |

### Reading the failure modes

These diagnostics matter more than the thresholds, because each one implies a different fix.

**Impressions rising, clicks at zero.** Pages are indexed and being read as irrelevant to the query. Titles and meta descriptions are the first suspect, page content the second. **Do not advance.** More pages of the same kind makes the problem larger, not smaller. Fix the template.

**Indexation stalled under 50%.** Google is crawling and declining. Either the pages are too similar to each other or internal linking is not surfacing them. **Do not advance.** Check the 60% uniqueness rule and the link graph. This is the single most dangerous signal in the whole framework.

**Service hubs ranking, city pages not.** The site is being read as a general services site rather than a local one. Strengthen the Local Ledger, add housing-stock specificity, and check that city pages are more than three clicks from the homepage.

**Everything green well before four weeks.** Still wait. Early indexation of a small phase does not predict a large one, and there is no prize for speed here.

**"Discovered, currently not indexed" climbing.** Crawl budget is exhausted. Stop. Reduce page count if necessary. Advancing at this point compounds the problem.

## 6. INTERNAL LINKING ARCHITECTURE

The link graph is the highest-leverage asset in a programmatic build and the easiest thing to break with a careless edit. Enforcement rules are in CLAUDE.md section 10. This section explains the shape and the reasoning.

### The silo, described

```
                         HOMEPAGE
                             |
        +--------------------+--------------------+
        |                    |                    |
   /services/           /locations/            /blog/
        |                    |                    |
   7 service hubs      3 state hubs         150 posts
        |                    |                    |
   38 sub-service      559 city hubs             |
   pages                    |                     |
        |                   |                     |
        +---------> 3,913 service x city <--------+
                    pages (deepest tier)
```

Two spines cross at the deepest tier. The **service spine** runs from `/services/roofing/` down to every roofing-in-a-city page. The **geographic spine** runs from `/locations/massachusetts/` down through `/locations/massachusetts/worcester/` to the same page. Every deep page has exactly two parents, one in each spine, and links up to both.

That crossing is the whole design. It tells Google that `/locations/massachusetts/worcester/roofing/` is simultaneously a roofing page and a Worcester page, which is exactly what the query "roofers Worcester MA" is asking for.

### Lateral linking, and why it carries the weight

Vertical links establish hierarchy. Lateral links do the work of distributing authority into the long tail, where almost all the traffic lives.

**Same service, nearby towns.** The Worcester roofing page links to roofing in Shrewsbury, Auburn, Holden, Millbury, and Leicester, sourced from `nearbyTowns`. This mirrors real search behavior, since a homeowner near a town line searches both, and it means a link earned by one city page distributes into its geographic neighbours.

**Same town, other services.** The Worcester roofing page links to Worcester windows and Worcester siding. A homeowner replacing a roof this year is a bathroom lead next year, and this is the structural expression of the seven-category advantage no competitor has.

**Grouping is mandatory.** A service hub must never render 559 links. Group by state, then by region, and collapse behind a disclosure. A flat 559-link block dilutes every link on the page and reads as a doorway.

### Blog integration

Blog posts are the primary link earners and they must feed the money pages. Every post links to its service hub in the first third of the body, not from a footer block, to its state hub, to two or three genuinely relevant city pages, to two or three sibling posts, and to a tool page when it discusses cost.

The location-feature listicles matter most here. "Best Roofing Contractors in Worcester MA" links directly into the Worcester roofing page and earns links from the businesses named in it.

### Absolute rules

- Never link to a city that is not live in the current phase. Every link filters through `getLiveCities()`.
- No orphans. Every generated page is reachable from at least two others.
- Breadcrumbs on every page below the homepage, matching the URL hierarchy exactly.
- Varied descriptive anchors. Never "click here." Never the same exact anchor site-wide.
- Nothing more than three clicks from the homepage.

## 7. BLOG CONTENT STRATEGY

### Why New England-specific content builds authority faster

The generic home improvement content space is saturated by national sites with enormous authority. Angi, HomeAdvisor, Bob Vila, and This Old House will outrank a new domain on "how much does a roof cost" indefinitely, and competing there is a waste of the first year.

New England-specific content is the opposite situation. Consider what those national sites cannot write credibly:

- **Ice dams.** A genuine regional phenomenon with a specific mechanism: heat escapes into an under-insulated attic, melts the snowpack, the meltwater refreezes at the cold overhang, and the resulting dam pushes water back up under the shingles. Verified volume 1,700 a month at difficulty 6. A national site writes 800 generic words. A regional site writes the mechanism, the insulation and ventilation fix, and what steam removal costs here.
- **Salt air.** Coastal Rhode Island, the Cape, the South Shore, and the Connecticut shoreline all have fastener corrosion and paint-life problems that do not exist in Ohio.
- **Triple deckers.** Roughly 15,000 in Worcester alone, plus Providence, Lowell, Lawrence, Somerville, and New Bedford. A window job means 40 to 60 openings across three floors of weight-and-pulley sash. That is a completely different project from a suburban window job and nobody writes about it.
- **Historic districts.** Newport, Nantucket, Marblehead, Litchfield, Old Wethersfield, Deerfield, Old King's Highway on the Cape. Each governs window profile, siding material, and sometimes paint color. Homeowners in these districts search specifically and find almost nothing.
- **State programs.** Mass Save, Energize CT, the Connecticut Green Bank. High commercial intent and genuinely local.

**The authority mechanic.** Topical authority accrues from covering a topic comprehensively, not from covering many topics shallowly. Fifty posts about New England housing stock and climate make this site the most complete resource on that subject anywhere, which is achievable. Fifty posts about home improvement generally make it the ten-thousandth such resource.

### Why listicles drive AI citation

The location-feature posts, "Best Roofing Contractors in Worcester MA" and its 47 siblings, are 32% of the queue on purpose.

They rank fastest on a new domain, because "best X in Y" SERPs are dominated by aggregators rather than by high-authority brands. They earn links, because businesses named in a list link to it. And critically, **they are the format AI answer engines pull from.** When someone asks an assistant for a roofer in Worcester, the model needs a source that has already ranked and compared local options. A structured, sourced list is directly usable. A contractor's own homepage is not.

`docs/competitors.md` documents that this market is nearly invisible in AI answer engines. That window is open now and will not stay open.

**Constraints, restated because they matter.** Accurate and fair descriptions. No fabricated ratings, review counts, or years in business. Include this site in the same neutral register as everyone else. Disclose plainly that the list is published by this site. Never claim a ranking methodology that is not actually applied.

### Cadence

Five posts a week, weekdays, generated by Gemini 2.5 Flash into Sanity as drafts. **Every draft is human-reviewed before publish.** The model will invent rebate amounts, permit fees, and code sections that sound entirely plausible. Verify every figure.

## 8. BACKLINK STRATEGY

**First, the caveat that should govern the budget.** General Construction RI has DR 22 and one organic visit a month. Adam Quenneville has DR 30 and 153. Berkeley Exteriors has DR 6 and 2,237. In this market, indexed relevant pages beat authority decisively. **Do not spend heavily on links in the first six months.** Spend on pages.

That said, links compound, and the targets below are genuinely reachable.

### Tier 1: local news and regional publications

Local outlets run home and garden and seasonal preparedness content and are receptive to a regional expert source with actual data. Targets include the Providence Journal, Rhode Island Monthly, the Boston Globe regional sections, Worcester Telegram and Gazette, MassLive, the Berkshire Eagle, the Cape Cod Times, the Hartford Courant, the New Haven Register, the Connecticut Mirror, and CT Insider.

The angle that works: seasonal preparedness with real numbers. Ice dam season in January, storm preparation before a named nor'easter, spring inspection in March. Pitch the cost data, since nobody else in this market publishes it.

### Tier 2: historical societies and preservation organizations

The strongest and most defensible link category, because the historic-home content is genuinely useful to their audiences and almost nobody else produces it.

Rhode Island: the Preservation Society of Newport County, the Providence Preservation Society, the Rhode Island Historical Society, the Newport Restoration Foundation. Massachusetts: Historic New England, the Massachusetts Historical Commission, Historic Deerfield, the Nantucket Preservation Trust, and the many town-level historical commissions. Connecticut: Connecticut Landmarks, the Connecticut Trust for Historic Preservation, and town historic district commissions.

Approach: offer genuinely useful guidance on window replacement, siding, and roofing within historic district constraints. Never pitch it as a link request.

### Tier 3: municipal and regional government

Many town websites maintain homeowner resource pages, and permitting and historic district pages sometimes link out to explanatory guidance. Low volume, high trust, and highly relevant to the exact city pages being built.

### Tier 4: real estate

Regional brokerages, real estate blogs, and home inspector sites. Cost data and pre-sale improvement content are natural fits. Local Realtor association market reports occasionally cite renovation cost sources.

### Tier 5: industry and trade

The Rhode Island Builders Association, the Home Builders and Remodelers Association of Massachusetts, the Home Builders and Remodelers Association of Connecticut, NARI chapters, and regional home shows.

### Tier 6: the listicle mechanic

The 48 location-feature posts are a link acquisition system on their own. A business named favourably in "Best Roofing Contractors in Worcester MA" has a direct incentive to link to it. This is low-effort and compounds naturally as the queue publishes.

### What to avoid

Paid link schemes, private blog networks, mass directory submissions, and reciprocal link exchanges. In a market where DR 6 is beating DR 30, the risk is not worth any of it.

## 9. GOOGLE BUSINESS PROFILE STRATEGY

### The honest answer first

**A referral business with no physical service location and no completed jobs is a poor fit for Google Business Profile, and attempting to force it is the fastest way to get the whole thing suspended.**

GBP eligibility requires a business that makes in-person contact with customers. This business does not. It routes leads to contractors who do. Creating profiles for a service area business that does not service the area is a guidelines violation, and Google's enforcement on fake or ineligible service area businesses in home services is aggressive and unforgiving.

### One profile or several

**One, if any.** A single profile at the actual business address, with a real service area defined, if and only if the operation genuinely qualifies.

**Never create multiple profiles for a multi-state service area.** No profile in Providence, one in Worcester, one in Hartford. That is the classic multi-location spam pattern, it is well detected, and a suspension cascades to any related profile. The temptation is significant because local pack visibility is valuable. It is not worth it.

### What actually works instead

**Local pack visibility is not the play here. Organic and AI citation are.** That is fortunate, because it is exactly what the 559-city architecture is built for. The local pack requires proximity and a verified profile. The organic results below it do not, and that is where a genuinely local page competes on merit.

**If the model evolves.** If the business ever acquires a contractor, or partners exclusively enough to represent them, GBP becomes viable through that entity at its real address. That is a business decision, not an SEO tactic.

**Where partner contractors have profiles**, encourage them to keep them accurate and well-reviewed. Their local pack presence and this site's organic presence are complementary rather than competing.

### Reviews for a business with no history

The constraint is real and there is no shortcut. **Never publish fabricated testimonials.** The three reviews currently in `content.ts` are marked placeholders precisely because publishing them as real would violate FTC endorsement rules and expose the business to liability.

What works: collect first-party reviews from homeowners after a successful referral, with a short automated follow-up, and publish them only once they exist and are verifiable. Add `Review` schema only at that point, never before. Until then, run the site without a reviews section rather than with a fake one.

### The two inflection points, and what causes them

**Month 8.** Deep-tier pages start ranking in volume rather than individually. The 500 pages deployed in months 4 and 5 clear the typical three-to-five-month maturation window at roughly the same time, and their aggregate arrival is visible as a step change rather than a slope.

### Risks to the model

- **Google core update.** The largest single risk. A scaled-content classifier hit would be severe. The 60% uniqueness rule and the Local Ledger are the mitigation, and they only work if enforced.

- **AI answer engines compressing click-through.** Real and worsening industry-wide. The listicle and citation strategy is the hedge, and it is partial.
- **Phase gate discipline.** The most likely failure is human. Advancing early because the numbers look promising is how these projects die.

### Model sensitivities, in order of importance

1. **Conversion rate.** The 2.5% assumption moves everything. At 1.5% the month 18 figure drops by 40%. At 4% it rises by 60%. This is the number to measure first and hardest.
2. **Lead value realization.** Estimated values assume contractors pay near market rate. Early on, with no track record, expect to accept less. Build the network before optimizing price.
3. **Phase gate discipline.** Every phase delayed by an extra month pushes the whole curve right by a month, and that is the correct trade every time.
4. **Category mix.** Kitchen and bathroom leads are worth roughly double gutter leads. Weighting content toward the higher-value categories improves revenue without changing traffic.

### The unit economics worth internalizing

Three thousand nine hundred and thirteen service-by-city pages. If the average page produces just **two organic sessions a month** at maturity, that is 7,826 sessions from the deep tier alone, which is more than the entire current competitive market combined. At 2.5% conversion, roughly 195 leads a month, worth roughly $14,600 at the $75 midpoint.

Two sessions per page per month is a low bar. Several of these pages will produce twenty.

That is the arithmetic behind the whole project, and it is also the reason page quality cannot slip: the model only works if the pages get indexed, and thin pages do not.
