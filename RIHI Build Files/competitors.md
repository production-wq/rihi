# competitors.md

Competitive research for the Rhode Island, Massachusetts, and Connecticut home improvement market. Read this file at the start of every session before writing any copy.

**Data source and date.** Domain Rating, organic traffic, organic keyword counts, and organic traffic value were pulled from the Ahrefs API in August 2026, United States index, subdomains mode. Keyword volumes and difficulty scores were pulled from Ahrefs Keywords Explorer, United States. Qualitative observations come from direct review of the live sites. Where a domain could not be confirmed, that is stated explicitly and no metrics are invented.

**A note on one metric.** The referring-domain counts returned by the batch endpoint clustered implausibly tightly (every competitor between roughly 450 and 750 regardless of Domain Rating, including sites with a DR under 1). That pattern does not survive scrutiny and the field has been excluded from the analysis below. Domain Rating, organic traffic, and organic keywords are consistent with each other and with manual inspection, and those are the figures used.

## THE HEADLINE FINDING

Across a three-state market of roughly 6 million people and 2.6 million housing units, the strongest organic performer among the eighteen named competitors gets about **3,000 organic visits a month** at a **Domain Rating of 14**.

Twelve of the sixteen measured competitors get under 400 organic visits a month. Four get under 50. Two get under 10.

For context on how low that bar is: a single well-optimized page on a mature site routinely does 3,000 visits a month by itself. The entire competitive set of this market, combined, produces roughly 7,700 monthly organic visits.

There is no incumbent. There is no one to displace. There is only an empty index.

## RHODE ISLAND

### RIHI (Rhode Island Home Improvement)
**Domain:** rihi.com
**DR 10 · 369 organic visits/mo · 67 keywords · $284/mo traffic value**

**Service focus.** The service taxonomy this project's structure was modeled on: roofing, windows, siding, bathrooms, kitchens, doors, gutters. Broad exterior plus interior remodeling.

**Geographic coverage.** Rhode Island, with some spillover into nearby Massachusetts. No page-level geographic segmentation.

**SEO strengths.** The exact-match domain does real work on branded and semi-branded queries. Clean service taxonomy. The name itself is close to the category term, which earns some direct traffic that will never be competed away.

**SEO weaknesses.** 67 ranking keywords is a rounding error for a business with statewide ambitions. There are 39 municipalities in Rhode Island and RIHI has roughly 1.7 ranking keywords per municipality. No city-level landing pages. No blog of consequence. Traffic value of $284 a month against a market where roofing clicks cost $14 tells you almost none of the traffic is transactional. This is a brochure site with a good domain name.

**Copy style.** Generic service descriptions. Heavy on "quality," "trusted," "experienced." Nothing tied to a specific street, neighborhood, or building type. The Providence triple-decker, the defining feature of Rhode Island's urban housing stock, does not appear.

### Couto Construction
**Domain:** coutoconstruction.com
**DR 14 · 3,027 organic visits/mo · 465 keywords · $15,294/mo traffic value**

**The one to actually study.** Couto is the market leader by a factor of 1.4 over the next competitor and by a factor of 8 over RIHI, and its traffic value is more than double the next site's. This is the only competitor in the set operating at a level that looks intentional.

**Service focus.** Roofing, siding, windows, decks. Exteriors, with a strong roofing lean.

**Geographic coverage.** Southeastern Massachusetts and Rhode Island, centered on the Fall River, New Bedford, and East Bay corridor. Not statewide in either state.

**SEO strengths.** 465 ranking keywords, roughly seven times RIHI. The keyword-to-traffic ratio suggests they rank for a genuine long tail rather than a handful of head terms. Traffic value of $15,294 a month means the traffic is commercial, not informational. They have clearly invested in content and it is working.

**SEO weaknesses.** Geographically capped. Their footprint is a corridor, not a region, and the site reflects that. Massachusetts coverage stops well short of Worcester and Boston. Connecticut coverage is effectively zero. This is the gap: they have proven the content model works in this market and then declined to scale it geographically.

**Copy style.** More specific than most of the set. Some genuine regional reference. Still leans on "craftsmanship" and "commitment."

**Threat rating: HIGH in southeastern Massachusetts and the RI East Bay. LOW everywhere else.** Do not lead the Rhode Island rollout with Fall River, Somerset, Swansea, Seekonk, Tiverton, or Little Compton. Lead with Providence County and Kent County, where Couto is weak, then approach the East Bay after domain authority exists.

### General Construction RI
**Domain:** generalconstructionri.com
**DR 22 · 1 organic visit/mo · 2 keywords · $0/mo traffic value**

**The most instructive entry in the table.** DR 22, one of the higher authority scores in the set, and a single organic visit a month. This is a site with links and no content. Authority without pages ranks for nothing.

**Takeaway for this project.** Do not over-index on backlink acquisition early. General Construction RI proves that authority alone produces zero traffic in this market. ### Jancon Exteriors
**Domain:** janconexteriors.com
**DR 4 · 162 organic visits/mo · 44 keywords · $552/mo traffic value**

**The most important competitor to understand structurally, because they are attempting the same play and executing it badly.**

**Service focus.** Roofing, siding, windows, decks, plus some interior. Based in Franklin, MA, licensed in MA and RI.

**Geographic coverage.** Eastern Massachusetts and northern Rhode Island.

**What they are doing.** Jancon has city pages. Real ones: `/woonsocket-ri-roofing-siding/`, `/medway-roofing-siding-windows-contractors/`, `/home-improvement-contractor-mansfield-ma/`. They understood the opportunity.

**Why it is not working, and these are the exact mistakes to avoid.**

1. **Flat URL structure.** Every city page sits at the root. There is no `/locations/` hierarchy, no state grouping, no parent-child relationship. Google gets no structural signal about how these pages relate, and no topical cluster forms.
2. **Inconsistent URL patterns.** One city is `-roofing-siding`, another is `-roofing-siding-windows-contractors`, another is `home-improvement-contractor-`. Three patterns across three pages means no template consistency and no scalable internal linking.
3. **Padding instead of specificity.** The Woonsocket page describes the town by population and names two parks: a conservation area and a memorial park. It says nothing about Woonsocket's housing stock, which is one of the densest concentrations of mill-era worker housing and triple-deckers in New England. The local detail is filler that happens to be local rather than information a homeowner needs.
4. **One page per city, not one page per service per city.** Woonsocket gets a single combined roofing-and-siding page. That page has to compete for "roofers Woonsocket RI" and "siding contractors Woonsocket RI" simultaneously and ranks well for neither.

**Result: 44 keywords and 162 visits a month.** They built the right idea with the wrong architecture.

**Threat rating: LOW as they stand, MEDIUM if they ever fix the structure.** They already have the pages. Restructuring is a weekend of work for a competent developer. The window is open but not permanently.

## MASSACHUSETTS

### Capital Construction Contracting
**Domain:** capitalconstructioncontracting.com
**DR 16 · 896 organic visits/mo · 165 keywords · $3,542/mo traffic value**

**The most sophisticated site in the entire set, and the most direct structural threat.**

**Service focus.** Roofing, siding, windows, decking, masonry. Exteriors only, no kitchens, no baths. Based in Somerville, operating since 2005.

**Geographic coverage.** Greater Boston primarily. The site claims Massachusetts, Rhode Island, New Hampshire, Connecticut, Vermont, and Maine, but the actual page coverage is Greater Boston.

**What they are doing right, and it is a lot.** Real city pages at a real URL pattern: `/service-areas/somerville`, `/service-areas/lexington`, `/service-areas/arlington`. Clean, consistent, hierarchical. And critically, the copy on those pages is genuinely local. The Lexington page references historic colonials near the Battle Green. The Arlington page names Jason Heights and Arlington Center. The Somerville page specifically discusses window upgrades in triple-deckers and flat roof replacements on compact lots.

**This is the only competitor writing city copy that could not be find-and-replaced.** Everything else in this market is a template. Capital Construction understood the assignment.

**Where they are beatable, and it is decisive.**

1. **Exteriors only.** No bathroom remodeling. No kitchen remodeling. Two of the seven service categories, and two of the highest-value ones, are simply absent. Tub-to-shower conversion alone is 6,400 monthly searches nationally at difficulty 3.
2. **Greater Boston only.** They have maybe a few dozen service-area pages against 351 Massachusetts municipalities. Worcester County, the Pioneer Valley, the Cape, the South Coast, and the Berkshires are open.
3. **One page per city, not per service per city.** Same architectural limit as Jancon. The Lexington page has to rank for roofing, siding, windows, decking, and masonry at once.
4. **165 keywords total.** Despite doing the work well, the footprint is small. Quality without scale.

**Threat rating: HIGH in Greater Boston across roofing, siding, and windows. ZERO in bathrooms and kitchens. ZERO outside Route 128.**

**Tactical implication.** Do not lead the Phase 2 Massachusetts rollout with Somerville, Cambridge, Arlington, Lexington, Medford, Malden, or Newton. Lead with Worcester, Springfield, New Bedford, Fall River, Brockton, Lynn, Lowell, and Lawrence, where the housing stock is dense, the search volume is real, and Capital Construction has no presence. Enter Greater Boston in Phase 4 with bathroom and kitchen pages first, where they cannot respond at all.

### Berkeley Exteriors
**Domain:** berkeleyexteriors.com
**DR 6 · 2,237 organic visits/mo · 617 keywords · $6,412/mo traffic value**

**The most important competitor in Connecticut and the second-largest organic performer in the entire market. Also the most interesting case study in the set.**

**Service focus.** Roofing, siding, windows, doors, patios, decks. Exteriors only. Operating since 1992.

**Geographic coverage.** Fairfield County and New Haven County primarily, with a service-area page claiming the whole state.

**What is remarkable.** **617 ranking keywords at a Domain Rating of 6.** That is the highest keyword count in the market, from the lowest-authority site among the top performers. Almost no backlinks and the widest keyword footprint. That combination only happens one way: page volume against near-zero competition.

**How they did it.** Their `/service-areas` page is a single page containing short paragraphs about dozens of Connecticut towns. Naugatuck gets a sentence about its historic district and McKim, Mead and White. New Canaan gets a Frank Lloyd Wright reference. New Fairfield gets the Cosier-Murphy House and the Williams House. North Branford gets the Northford Center Historic District.

**The critical weakness, and it is the entire opening for this project.** Every one of those paragraphs ends in the same phrase about top-quality exterior building products. They are one-paragraph mentions on a single shared page, not individual town pages. There is no `/service-areas/naugatuck/` URL. There is no separate roofing page for Naugatuck. Every town shares one URL, so every town competes with every other town for the same single ranking slot, and no town gets a page that can rank for its own transactional query.

**What this proves, and it is the strategic keystone of this entire project.** Berkeley Exteriors ranks for 617 keywords at DR 6 using nothing but *mentions* of Connecticut towns on one shared page. If a single page of town paragraphs generates 617 keywords and 2,237 visits at DR 6, then 169 dedicated town pages, each with seven dedicated service pages beneath them, each with real housing stock detail and a real Local Ledger, should substantially exceed that. The proof of concept has already been run by a competitor, accidentally, with the weakest possible execution.

**Threat rating: HIGH for Fairfield and New Haven County exteriors. ZERO for baths and kitchens. ZERO in Hartford, Litchfield, Windham, Tolland, and New London counties.**

**Tactical implication for Phase 3.** Enter Connecticut through Hartford County, the Quiet Corner, New London County, and Litchfield County first. Add bathroom and kitchen pages in Fairfield County immediately, since Berkeley cannot respond in categories they do not offer. Approach Fairfield County exterior terms last, once authority exists.

## SYNTHESIS

### The market SEO gap, stated precisely

Every competitor fails on at least one of three axes, and most fail on two or three.

**Axis 1: Geographic limitation.** Couto covers a southeastern MA and East Bay RI corridor. Capital Construction covers Greater Boston. Berkeley covers Fairfield and New Haven. Adam Quenneville covers the Pioneer Valley. Tom Curren covers six affluent MetroWest towns. Vinylume covers eight towns in the Fairfield panhandle. Not one competitor covers a full state. Not one covers two states with real page-level depth. None covers three.

**Axis 2: Service limitation.** Capital Construction, Berkeley Exteriors, Adam Quenneville, Royal Exteriors, Harmony, Nu-Face, and Jancon are exteriors-only. Tom Curren, Sunwood, and Ricci are interiors and design-build only. Sam Karam covers all seven and has two ranking keywords. **Nobody covers all seven service categories with any search presence at all.** Bathroom and kitchen remodeling are the most consistently absent, and tub-to-shower conversion alone carries 6,400 monthly searches at difficulty 3.

**Axis 3: No city-level page architecture.** This is the largest gap. Three competitors have attempted it. Jancon built flat, inconsistent URLs, one per city, covering both roofing and siding on the same page. Capital Construction built a clean `/service-areas/[city]` pattern with genuinely good local copy, then stopped at a few dozen Greater Boston towns and put all five services on each page. Berkeley put every town on one shared page as a paragraph each. **Nobody has built `/locations/[state]/[city]/[service]/`. Nobody has one page per service per city. That structure does not exist anywhere in this three-state market.**

**The gap, in one sentence.** No site in Rhode Island, Massachusetts, or Connecticut offers all seven home improvement service categories with a dedicated, genuinely local page for each service in each of 559 municipalities, and the closest attempt, Berkeley Exteriors, is ranking for 617 keywords at Domain Rating 6 using nothing but one-paragraph town mentions on a single shared page.

### Sequencing derived from the competitive map

**Phase 1, Rhode Island.** Lead with Providence County and Kent County. Providence, Cranston, Warwick, Pawtucket, Woonsocket, East Providence, North Providence, Central Falls, Johnston, Cumberland, Lincoln, West Warwick, Coventry. Couto is weak here and everyone else is invisible. Hold the East Bay towns, Bristol, Warren, Barrington, Tiverton, Little Compton, until Couto's actual strength there can be measured. **Phase 2, Massachusetts top 50.** Lead with Worcester, Springfield, New Bedford, Fall River, Brockton, Lynn, Lowell, Lawrence, Haverhill, Chicopee, Pittsfield, Fitchburg, Leominster, Taunton, Attleboro. Dense older housing stock, real volume, no meaningful incumbent. ### What differentiates this site's copy and positioning

**One.** Seven categories in one place. A homeowner replacing a roof this year and a bathroom next year finds both here.

**Two.** Actual coverage of all 559 municipalities, not a service-area page that names five towns and then says "and more."

**Three.** One page per service per city. Berkeley proved town mentions rank. Dedicated pages rank harder.

**Four.** Housing-stock specificity as the core content asset. Capital Construction is the only competitor doing this and only in Greater Boston. A Worcester triple-decker window job is a genuinely different job from a Wellesley garrison colonial window job: different opening counts, different sash mechanisms, different staging, different price per opening. Writing that difference is both better content and better SEO, and it is the one thing a competitor cannot copy quickly, because it requires knowing 559 municipalities rather than owning a template.

**Five.** Cost transparency. Nobody in this market publishes numbers. Every competitor says "free estimate" and stops. Publishing real ranges captures the entire cost-guide search category, which is the largest informational segment in the blog queue.

**Six.** Honest referral framing. The site does not pretend to be a contractor. That constraint turns out to be an advantage: it licenses genuinely neutral comparison content, including material comparisons and cost guides, that a contractor selling one product line cannot write credibly.

## TACTICAL INSTRUCTIONS FOR WRITING ANY PAGE ON THIS SITE

Follow these on every page. They are derived directly from the failures documented above.

**1. Open with a fact, not with the brand.** The first sentence of any page body should state something true about the reader's situation, house, or town. Never "Fieldstone Home Improvement is proud to serve..." Nine competitors open that way and it forfeits the first sentence.

**2. Name the housing stock in the first hundred words of any city or city-service page.** Not "homes in this area." The actual stock: triple-deckers, garrison colonials, postwar capes, center chimney colonials, mill worker housing, shingled coastal cottages. If the `homeStyleNote` for that city does not give you enough to write from, research it or do not publish the page.

**3. Put a number in every section.** Cost range, timeline in days, roof pitch, opening count, R-value, years of paint life, distance ice and water shield runs up from the eave. Every competitor writes in adjectives. Numbers are both more useful and more differentiated, and they are what cost-guide searchers are looking for.

**4. Never make a claim the business cannot make.** Not licensed, not insured, no crews, no certifications, no warranty, no years in business, no project counts. Reread section 8 of CLAUDE.md before writing any trust-related copy.

**5. Write the mechanism, not the drama.** Not "harsh New England winters take a toll." Instead: heat escaping through an under-insulated attic melts the snowpack, the meltwater refreezes at the cold overhang, and the resulting dam pushes water back up under the shingles. One of those sentences is worth reading.

**6. Sixty percent of any service x city page must be unspecific-to-nothing-else.** Before publishing, apply this test: could this paragraph be pasted onto the page for a different city with only the city name changed? If yes for more than 40 percent of the body, the page is not ready. This is the single test that separates this project from Jancon's.

**7. Never pad with geography trivia.** Jancon's Woonsocket page names a conservation area and a memorial park. That is not local relevance, it is local-flavored filler. Population figures, park names, and highway numbers do not help a homeowner decide about a roof. Housing stock, permitting authority, and cost do.

**8. Comparison content should be genuinely neutral.** The referral model means there is no product line to defend. Say when vinyl is the right answer. Say when a repair beats a replacement. Say when a project is not worth doing this year. Every competitor is a contractor who needs to sell the job; that is a credibility gap this site can walk straight through.

**9. Vary sentence length deliberately.** Competitor copy is uniformly medium-length declarative sentences, which is also the default rhythm of generated text. Short sentences carry weight. Then a longer one that carries the detail, the qualification, and the number that makes the short sentence credible.

**10. Read the sentence back and ask whether a contractor would say it out loud at a kitchen table.** "We are committed to elevating the standard of exterior remodeling" fails. "On a house that age the sheathing is usually board, not plywood, so budget for a day of prep you might not need on a newer roof" passes.
